Merchant of Record vs DIY Stripe: What You Really Keep in 2026

Headline Stripe looks 40% cheaper than a Merchant of Record until recurring billing, automated tax calculation, and cross-border FX surcharges stack on top of small SaaS renewals.

Why 2.9% + $0.30 rarely matches a SaaS bank payout

Most pricing comparisons pit Stripe base card processing (2.9% + $0.30 in the US) directly against Merchant of Record (MoR) rates such as Polar.sh (4.0% + $0.40) or Paddle (5.0% + $0.50). On paper, raw Stripe looks like an easy win.

That comparison breaks down the moment a software founder sells recurring subscriptions to buyers outside the United States. Unless you build custom subscription scheduling, dunning emails, and multi-jurisdiction tax logic against raw PaymentIntents, four extra Stripe fees stack on top of the base card rate.

  • Stripe Billing for recurring subscriptions adds +0.7% of billing volume.
  • Stripe Tax automated rate lookup adds +0.5% per transaction.
  • Non-US buyer cards trigger a +1.5% international card surcharge.
  • Settling foreign currencies into a USD bank account adds a +1.0% FX fee.

Tax liability distinction

Paying +0.5% for Stripe Tax only calculates the tax amount at checkout. You still have to register, file returns, and remit VAT or GST in each country once you pass local thresholds (which is €0 for non-EU SaaS sellers selling to EU consumers).

Side-by-side payout table on $4,350 monthly SaaS revenue

Consider a typical indie SaaS processing 150 monthly renewals at $29/month ($4,350 gross MRR), with 60% US buyers and 40% international buyers.

Newer developer-focused MoRs launched over the past two years (Polar.sh, Creem.io, and Dodo Payments) have compressed MoR pricing from 5% down to roughly 4%, closing the gap with DIY Stripe completely at sub-$50 ticket sizes.

The exact revenue profile where direct Stripe pulls ahead

Direct Stripe is still the right choice once average contract values rise or buyer geography concentrates in one domestic market. On a $299/month B2B plan paid mostly by US companies via domestic cards or ACH, fixed cent fees become negligible and hiring an accountant for state sales tax nexus costs far less than giving up 4% to 5% of gross revenue.

Common questions

Do Polar.sh, Creem, and Paddle handle EU VAT from the first euro?

Yes. Because a Merchant of Record acts as the legal reseller on the buyer receipt, EU VAT, UK VAT, and US state sales tax collection and remittance are handled under their tax registrations from transaction one.

Why does Lemon Squeezy cost more than Paddle on non-US SaaS sales?

Both advertise 5% + $0.50, but Lemon Squeezy adds +1.5% on non-US cards and +0.5% on recurring subscriptions, whereas Paddle keeps its 5% + $0.50 rate flat across cross-border cards.
