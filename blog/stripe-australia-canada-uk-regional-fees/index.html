Why Domestic Stripe Fees Range From 1.5% to 2.9% Across AU, UK, EU, CA & US

Interchange regulations make domestic Stripe processing nearly 50% cheaper in the UK, Europe, and Australia than in the US and Canada, until cross-border cards hit.

Domestic vs international Stripe rates across five major regions

Because consumer credit card interchange fees are capped by regulators in the United Kingdom, European Economic Area, and Australia, Stripe’s published domestic rates in those markets sit far below North American rates.

The catch for Australian, Canadian, UK, and European SaaS founders is that selling in USD to American customers triggers both cross-border card surcharges and a 2.0% currency conversion fee unless you attach a matching multi-currency settlement account.

Eliminating the 2.0% Stripe currency conversion fee in AU, CA, UK, and EU

Non-US Stripe accounts pay a 2.0% FX conversion surcharge (double the 1.0% US FX fee) whenever buyer charge currency differs from your bank payout currency.

In Canada, the UK, and Europe, attaching a local USD currency account (such as a Wise Business USD routing number where supported by Stripe multi-currency settlement) lets you settle USD charges directly in USD without triggering Stripe’s automatic 2.0% conversion spread.

Common questions

Why is Stripe Australia 1.7% + A$0.30 on domestic cards but 3.5% on international cards?

Reserve Bank of Australia (RBA) interchange caps keep domestic Australian Visa and Mastercard costs low (1.70% + A$0.30 on Stripe AU), whereas cards issued outside Australia fall outside domestic interchange caps and are billed at 3.50% + A$0.30 (plus 2.0% if currency conversion is required).

Does Stripe Australia include 10% GST in its fee?

Stripe Australia charges 10% GST on top of the processing fee itself (not on your gross sale), which GST-registered Australian businesses can claim back as an input tax credit on their BAS.
